# Reveal — Brand Brief

## Purpose
In the live moment of worship, the spoken word is met by the written word —
near-instantly, without anyone scrambling, fumbling a login, or losing the
thread. Reveal earns trust by **disappearing**: the technology recedes so the
service can lead.

## Audience (who the brand serves)
- **Primary stakeholder — the operator** (AV lead / worship-tech volunteer,
  often the person running sound in a small church). They adopt, set up, and
  run Reveal under live pressure at 10am. If it frays *their* nerves, Reveal is
  dead in that church. The brand's first job is to make them feel: *"I'm in
  control. Nothing slipped. This respects the service."*
- **Champion / buyer — the church.** The community that decides to adopt Reveal
  needs to feel it is low-risk, trustworthy, and free of IT headache.
- **The congregation — the experience we craft, not a stakeholder we serve.**
  They can't adopt Reveal; they only receive it. Their "wow" is our emotional
  north star, but they are not the audience the product is *designed for*.

## Personality
Contemporary. Effortless. Quietly premium. Human.
**Modern and easy** — not solemn-old-church, not startup-corporate, not a
design-YouTuber voice. Reveal is its own brand with its own calm confidence.

## Tone of voice
- Plain, warm, unhurried. Short sentences. No church-jargon for its own sake,
  no tech-bro hype.
- Reassuring over impressive. "You've got this" not "Behold our AI."
- Specific and faithful — names the passage, honors the reading, never
  paraphrases away the weight.

## Emotional promise
Five words: **"I was part of the service."**
The worshipper's takeaway: church was good, and my experience was even better
because I could contribute / follow along using this calm, capable system. (The
"operating system" is a *positioning metaphor* — Reveal as the quiet OS for the
service — not a word we put in users' faces.)

## How it should feel (the non-physical spec)
- **Calm under pressure.** The operator feels steadied, not hurried.
- **Quietly in control.** Active things are lit; idle things recede. Nothing
  shouts.
- **Faithful.** The word is honored exactly; the tool never shows off.
- **Effortless to the room.** The congregation feels included, not performed
  at.
- **Modern but reverent.** Contemporary craft that respects the moment it
  serves.

## Visual language as brand expression (not just decoration)
Our existing design language *is* the brand made visible — name it so future
work stays coherent:
- Oceanic Midnight palette = stillness, depth, focus.
- Cyan accent **only on what's live/active** = "here is the word, now."
- Dimmed idle, BorderBeam on the current item = attention follows the moment.
- Calm motion (Emil bar: <300ms, transform/opacity, reduced-motion) =
  competence without spectacle.

## Never this (guardrails)
- Never gimmicky, never "look what we can do."
- Never corporate-church or stale-hymnal.
- Never makes the operator feel behind or stupid.
- Never treats the congregation as a target to impress.
- Never lets the tech upstage the service.
- Never position design/prettiness as the wedge — the wedge is zero-install PWA,
  congregant follow-along, liturgical mode.
